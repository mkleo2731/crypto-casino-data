How Crash Games Work: Algorithm Analysis and Strategy Math

By Leo · 2026-05-10

What Is a Crash Game?

A crash game is a multiplier-based betting format where a coefficient starts at 1.00x and rises continuously until it "crashes" at a randomly determined point. Players place a bet before the round begins and must cash out before the crash — if they do, their bet is multiplied by the current coefficient; if they don't, they lose the entire wager. The mechanic is pure math: no cards, no reels, no opponent. Just a curve, a threshold, and a decision.

The Algorithm: Hash Chains and Crash Point Derivation

Provably fair crash games determine every outcome before the game begins using a hash chain seeded from a single server seed. The process works as follows:

1. Generate the hash chain. The operator picks a server seed and hashes it with SHA-256 iteratively — typically 10 million times. The final hash is published as a commitment. Each game round uses hashes in reverse order (from the end of the chain backward), so the operator cannot alter future outcomes without breaking the chain.

seed_0 = server_seed
seed_n = SHA-256(seed_{n-1})   // repeat N times
publish: seed_N (commitment)
game 1 uses seed_{N-1}, game 2 uses seed_{N-2}, ...

2. Incorporate a public/player seed. To prevent the operator from pre-selecting favorable chains, the round hash is combined with a public seed (often a Bitcoin block hash chosen after the chain is committed):

hmac = HMAC-SHA256(round_hash, public_seed)

3. Derive the crash point. The first 52 bits (13 hex characters) of the HMAC output are converted to an integer, and the crash multiplier is calculated:

hash_value = int(hmac[:13], 16)   // first 52 bits
crash_point = max(1, floor(2^52 / (hash_value + 1) * (1 - house_edge)))

This produces a distribution where the probability of the game surviving to multiplier m is approximately (1 - house_edge) / m. The house edge is baked directly into the crash point formula — every outcome is shifted slightly downward.

Why 52 bits? JavaScript (the runtime for most crash game clients) uses 64-bit IEEE 754 floats, which have 52 bits of mantissa precision. Using exactly 52 bits of the hash avoids floating-point rounding errors that could create exploitable biases.

House Edge Math

The house edge in crash games is applied multiplicatively to every crash point. A 3% edge means the expected crash point distribution is scaled by 0.97. In practice, this manifests as roughly 1 in 33 rounds crashing instantly at 1.00x (an automatic loss regardless of strategy).

The probability of an instant crash (multiplier = 1.00x) equals the house edge itself. For a 3% edge, ~3% of rounds end immediately.

Lower house edge means higher theoretical RTP (return to player). At 1%, the RTP is 99% — competitive with the best table games in traditional casinos.

Expected Value at Different Cash-Out Targets

No matter where you set your auto-cashout, the expected value (EV) per unit wagered is always -house_edge. What changes is the variance — higher targets hit less often but pay more when they do.

For a 1% house edge, the probability of reaching multiplier m is 0.99 / m:

Higher targets mean longer losing streaks punctuated by larger wins, with the same long-run cost.

Strategy Analysis

Martingale (Double After Loss)

The Martingale strategy — doubling your bet after each loss at a 2x cashout target — is the most common system applied to crash games. The math is straightforward: after n consecutive losses, you need 2^n times your base bet to recover. At a 2x target with 1% house edge, the probability of losing k rounds in a row is (0.505)^k.

  • 5 consecutive losses: 3.3% chance, requires 32x base bet
  • 10 consecutive losses: 0.11% chance, requires 1,024x base bet
  • 15 consecutive losses: 0.0035% chance, requires 32,768x base bet

The problem isn't probability — it's bankroll. A $1 base bet Martingale needs a $32,768 bankroll to survive 15 losses. And even with that bankroll, the maximum profit from the recovery is still just $1. The risk/reward ratio inverts exponentially. Over enough rounds, a losing streak will exceed your bankroll, and the single catastrophic loss wipes out all accumulated small wins.

Fixed Cashout (Flat Betting)

Setting a fixed cashout target and betting a constant amount is the baseline strategy. At 2x with a 1% edge, you win 49.5% of rounds and lose 50.5%. Over 1,000 rounds at $1/bet, your expected loss is $10 (1% of $1,000 wagered) with a standard deviation of ~$22.36. This approach keeps variance bounded and losses predictable.

Kelly Criterion Applied to Crash Games

The Kelly criterion optimizes bet sizing for positive-EV situations. Since crash games have negative EV, Kelly formally recommends a bet size of zero. However, if you're playing regardless (for entertainment), a fractional Kelly approach can minimize ruin probability: bet a fixed small percentage of your bankroll (1-2%) at a moderate cashout target (2-3x). This maximizes play time while keeping the expected cost linear and manageable.

Verification: Don't Trust, Verify

The entire point of provably fair crash games is that you can verify every outcome independently. After a game ends, the platform reveals the round hash. You can then:

  1. Hash it with SHA-256 to confirm it produces the previous round's hash (chain integrity)
  2. Combine it with the public seed via HMAC-SHA256
  3. Apply the crash point formula to confirm the displayed multiplier matches

The open-source Provably Fair Verifier tool automates this process — feed it a game's hash and seed data and it recalculates the crash point independently.

If the hash chain breaks at any point — meaning SHA-256(hash_n) does not equal hash_{n+1} — the operator has tampered with the sequence, and every outcome after the break is suspect.

Conclusion

Crash games are mathematically transparent in a way most gambling formats are not. The hash chain mechanism makes outcome manipulation detectable, and the crash point formula is simple enough to verify with a few lines of code. But transparency doesn't change the economics: the house edge guarantees negative EV on every bet, and no betting strategy can overcome that. The value of understanding the math isn't finding an edge — it's knowing exactly what you're paying for the entertainment.
