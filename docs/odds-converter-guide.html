Free Odds Converter Guide: Decimal, Fractional & American

By Leo · 2026-05-10

Why Odds Conversion Matters

Different sportsbooks display odds in different formats, and crypto betting platforms almost always default to decimal. If you are used to fractional odds from UK bookmakers or American odds from US-based platforms, you need to convert before you can compare lines or calculate your true edge. A reliable odds converter eliminates guesswork and lets you spot value across any sportsbook in seconds.

The Three Major Odds Formats

Decimal Odds

Decimal odds represent the total return per unit staked, including your original stake.

  • Example: Odds of 2.50 mean you receive $2.50 for every $1.00 wagered. Your profit is $1.50, and your original $1.00 is returned.
  • How to read them: Multiply your stake by the decimal number. That is your total payout.
  • Where they are used: Europe, Australia, Canada, and nearly every crypto sportsbook (Stake, BC.Game, Cloudbet, 1xBit, and others).

Decimal odds are the simplest format. Any number above 2.00 means the potential profit exceeds your stake.

Fractional Odds

Fractional odds show profit relative to your stake as a ratio.

  • Example: Odds of 3/2 mean you win $3 in profit for every $2 staked. Your total return is $5 (profit + stake).
  • How to read them: The left number is profit; the right number is stake.
  • Where they are used: United Kingdom, Ireland, and traditional horse racing markets.

American Odds (Moneyline)

American odds use positive and negative numbers anchored to $100.

  • Positive (+): How much profit you make on a $100 stake. +150 means $150 profit on a $100 bet.
  • Negative (-): How much you need to stake to win $100 profit. -200 means you must wager $200 to win $100.
  • Where they are used: United States, some Canadian sportsbooks.

The further a negative number is from zero, the heavier the favorite. The larger the positive number, the bigger the underdog.

Conversion Formulas

Decimal to American:

If decimal >= 2.00:  American = (decimal - 1) x 100       → positive
If decimal < 2.00:   American = -100 / (decimal - 1)      → negative

American to Decimal:

If American > 0:  Decimal = (American / 100) + 1
If American < 0:  Decimal = (100 / |American|) + 1

Decimal to Fractional:

Fractional = (decimal - 1) / 1    →  simplify the fraction
Example: 2.50 → 1.5/1 → 3/2

Implied Probability:

Implied Probability = (1 / decimal odds) x 100
Example: 1 / 2.50 = 0.40 → 40%

Implied probability tells you what the sportsbook believes is the true chance of an outcome. If your own estimate is higher than the implied probability, the bet has positive expected value.

Example 3: Calculating Expected Value (EV)

You estimate Argentina has a 30% chance of winning their match. A sportsbook offers decimal odds of 4.00 (implied probability: 25%).

EV formula:

EV = (Your Probability x Profit) - (Probability of Losing x Stake)
EV = (0.30 x $300) - (0.70 x $100)
EV = $90 - $70 = +$20

Building Your Own Converter

If you want a command-line tool for batch conversions or integration into your own scripts, the crypto-betting-odds repository provides an open-source CLI odds converter. Here is a minimal Python snippet for decimal and American conversion:

def decimal_to_american(decimal_odds):
    if decimal_odds >= 2.0:
        return f"+{round((decimal_odds - 1) * 100)}"
    else:
        return f"{round(-100 / (decimal_odds - 1))}"

def american_to_decimal(american_odds):
    if american_odds > 0:
        return round((american_odds / 100) + 1, 2)
    else:
        return round((100 / abs(american_odds)) + 1, 2)

# Usage
print(decimal_to_american(2.50))   # +150
print(american_to_decimal(-200))   # 1.5
