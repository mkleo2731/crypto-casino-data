The Math Behind Crypto Betting: Odds, Parlays, and the Kelly Criterion

Published May 10, 2026 • 2,461 words

Most bettors lose money because they don't understand the math. Not the gambling — the math. Understanding odds conversion, expected value, and optimal stake sizing is the difference between gambling and investing.

This guide covers the essential mathematics every crypto bettor should know.

Odds Formats: The Same Thing, Three Ways

Betting odds come in three formats that all express the same information:

FormatExampleMeaning
Decimal2.50Bet 1, get 2.50 back (1.50 profit)
American+150Bet 100, profit 150
Fractional3/2Bet 2, profit 3

Conversion Formulas

Decimal to American:


If decimal >= 2.0: American = (decimal - 1) × 100
If decimal < 2.0:  American = -100 / (decimal - 1)

Decimal to Implied Probability:


probability = 1 / decimal × 100%

So decimal odds of 2.50 imply a 40% chance (1/2.50).

Parlay Mathematics

A parlay combines multiple bets into one. The combined odds multiply:


parlayOdds = leg1 × leg2 × leg3 × ... × legN

Example: A 4-leg parlay at 1.91, 2.10, 1.75, and 2.40:


combined = 1.91 × 2.10 × 1.75 × 2.40 = 16.84

A 0.01 BTC stake returns 0.1684 BTC — that's a 1,584% return. Sounds great, right?

The Parlay Trap

Here's what most bettors miss. The implied probability of hitting all four legs:


probability = (1/1.91) × (1/2.10) × (1/1.75) × (1/2.40)
            = 0.523 × 0.476 × 0.571 × 0.417
            = 5.3%

You'll hit this parlay roughly 1 in 19 times. The payout multiplier is 16.84x, but you need 18.87x just to break even (1/0.053). The house edge on parlays is typically 15-30%.

Expected Value (EV): The Only Metric That Matters

Expected value tells you if a bet is profitable long-term:


EV = (probability × profit) - ((1 - probability) × stake)

Positive EV example:

  • You estimate Team A has a 60% chance to win
  • The odds are 2.10 (implied probability: 47.6%)
  • Stake: 0.01 BTC
    • 
EV = (0.60 × 0.011) - (0.40 × 0.01) = 0.0066 - 0.004 = +0.0026 BTC

This bet has a positive expected value of 0.0026 BTC per bet. Over 100 bets, you'd expect to profit ~0.26 BTC.

The Kelly Criterion: Optimal Stake Sizing

Knowing a bet is +EV isn't enough — you need to know how much to bet. The Kelly Criterion tells you the mathematically optimal stake:


kellyFraction = (probability × (decimal - 1) - (1 - probability)) / (decimal - 1)

Example:

  • 60% edge, 2.10 odds:
    • 
kelly = (0.60 × 1.10 - 0.40) / 1.10 = 0.26 / 1.10 = 23.6% of bankroll

Fractional Kelly

Full Kelly is aggressive. Most professional bettors use fractional Kelly:

StrategyFractionRisk Level
Full Kelly100%Very aggressive
Half Kelly50%Moderate
Quarter Kelly25%Conservative

For crypto betting where bankroll volatility is already high (crypto price fluctuations), quarter Kelly is recommended.

Crypto-Specific Calculations

When betting with cryptocurrency, you need to account for the crypto's price:


btcPayout = stake × odds
usdValue = btcPayout × currentBTCPrice

If BTC is at $95,000 and you bet 0.01 BTC at 2.50 odds:

  • BTC payout: 0.025 BTC
  • USD value: $2,375
  • Profit in USD: $1,425

But if BTC drops 10% before you cash out, your profit shrinks to $1,187.50. This is why stablecoin betting (USDT, USDC) reduces variance.

Hedging with Crypto

Hedging lets you lock in profit regardless of outcome:


layStake = (backStake × backOdds) / layOdds
guaranteedProfit = backStake × (backOdds - 1) - layStake × (layOdds - 1)

This is especially useful in prediction markets where odds shift dramatically.

Key Takeaways

  1. Convert odds to implied probability before betting — if your estimated probability exceeds the implied probability, the bet has positive EV
  2. Parlays are entertainment, not strategy — the house edge compounds with each leg
  3. Use Kelly criterion (fractional) to size your bets — never go all-in on a single bet
  4. Account for crypto volatility — stablecoins reduce variance if you're betting for profit, not speculation
  5. Track your EV, not your results — small sample results are noise, EV is signal
